## The Core Setup

In standard successor representations, we learn $M(s, s') = \mathbb{E}[\sum_{t=0}^{\infty} \gamma^t \mathbb{1}(s_{t} = s') | s_0 = s]$. 

Your proposal is to define SRs over **conditional probability graphs of inference classes** — so the "states" aren't raw observations but *types of inference* (e.g., perceptual, predictive, counterfactual, etc.), and transitions between them are conditioned on task structure.

## The N-Back Symmetry You're Identifying

Standard n-back: "Press when current stimulus matches the one from *n steps ago*" (backward-looking, retrospective match).

Your proposed symmetries:

| Variant | Operation | What it learns |
|--------|-----------|--------------|
| **n-back** | Match $s_t$ with $s_{t-n}$ | SR over *past* dependencies; encodes "where did I come from" |
| **n+fwd** (your n+1, n+2) | Match $s_t$ with predicted $s_{t+n}$ | SR over *future* dependencies; encodes "where am I going" |
| **Improbability/violation** | Press when $P(s_t \| \text{context})$ is low | SR over *surprise* or *model violation*; encodes "what's anomalous" |

## Why This Is More Than a Task Design

The key insight: **these are all valid bases for SRs, but they learn different *geometries* over the inference graph.**

### 1. Backward SR (standard n-back)
Learns $M^{\leftarrow}$ where $M^{\leftarrow}_{ij}$ = expected future visits to inference-class $j$ starting from $i$, looking backward. This is the "credit assignment" geometry — which past inferences predict current ones.

### 2. Forward SR (your n+1, n+2 match)
Learns $M^{\rightarrow}$ where the prediction target is future rather than past. But here's the subtlety: **in a stationary Markov chain, $M^{\rightarrow}$ and $M^{\leftarrow}$ are related by the stationary distribution** — they're not independent. 

However, if your inference classes have *directed* transitions (A enables B but not vice versa), then $M^{\rightarrow}$ captures affordances/enablement relations while $M^{\leftarrow}$ captures explanation/cause relations. **This is the symmetry you're after: forward SRs encode "what this inference makes possible"; backward SRs encode "what made this inference possible."**

### 3. Violation-SR (improbability detection)
This is different. Instead of matching specific stimuli, you're matching *prediction errors* — pressing when the generative model of the sequence is violated. 

This trains an SR over **belief states about the model itself**. The "successor" here isn't "what stimulus comes next" but "what model revision becomes necessary." This is an SR over **meta-inference transitions** — moving from "model holds" to "model fails" to "model updates."

## A Unified Training Paradigm

If you want to use the *same* conditional probability graphs for both:

**Graph nodes** = inference classes (or their belief states)
**Graph edges** = conditional probabilities $P(\text{inference}_j | \text{inference}_i, \text{context})$

Then the three SR variants correspond to **three different reward functions** on the same graph:

| SR Type | Reward function $r(s')$ | What accumulates |
|--------|------------------------|------------------|
| Backward | $\mathbb{1}(s' = \text{past target})$ | Retrospective binding strength |
| Forward | $\mathbb{1}(s' = \text{future predicted})$ | Prospective affordance strength |
| Violation | $-\log P(s' \| \text{model})$ | Expected surprise / information gain |

## The Deep Claim

Your "symmetry of n-back" is actually a claim about **temporal representation in inference graphs**: 

> *The brain (or model) doesn't just learn "what follows what" (forward) or "what preceded what" (backward), but also "what violates what" (model-breaking). These are three orthogonal geometries over the same underlying transition structure.*

In the Trident G framework (from your earlier work), this maps cleanly:
- **Backward SR** → τ-trace (what constraints bound the past?)
- **Forward SR** → Ψ-band prospective control (what states are reachable?)
- **Violation SR** → Meta-monitoring / Phase 0 gating (when does the model itself need updating?)

## The Training Protocol

```
Given: Conditional probability graph G over inference classes
For each trial:
    1. Present sequence generated by G
    2. With probability p, sample task type:
       - BACKWARD: cue match to s_{t-n}
       - FORWARD: cue match to predicted s_{t+n}  
       - VIOLATION: cue when P(s_t|history) < threshold
    3. Train SR with TD error on the relevant reward
    4. Share the same G for all three — only the reward/attention filter changes
```
The shared G enforces that all three SRs live in the same representational space, but they partition the geometry differently. The "portable Y variable" (from your Trident G work) is exactly what's preserved across these three geometries — the invariant structure of the inference graph itself.
