# IQ Coach Partner Brief

## Future Skills Reasoning Pilot for South Africa

### AI-Era Judgement, Problem Solving and Transferable Thinking for Learners, Graduates and Early-Career Professionals

**Audience:** Government skills programmes, SETA-linked training providers, youth-employability organisations, universities, TVET and CET partners, corporate L&D teams, AI-skills providers, graduate-development programmes, NGOs, education partners and workforce-development funders.

**Positioning:** A non-clinical, non-selection reasoning and thinking-skills pilot for South African education, employability and workforce-development contexts.

**Core proposition:** IQ Coach helps learners, graduates and professionals practise the thinking skills needed for the AI economy: focus, working memory, pattern recognition, problem solving, evidence checking, AI-output judgement and reasoning across new situations.

**Suggested South Africa offer name:** Future Skills Reasoning Pilot
**Platform name:** IQ Coach by IQ Mindware
**Protocol:** Trident-G Far Transfer Protocol

---

## Executive Summary

South Africa does not only need more digital tools. It needs more people who can think well with those tools.

AI, automation and digital platforms are changing work, learning and employability. Technical skills matter, but they are not enough. Learners, graduates, workers and jobseekers also need the human thinking skills that make digital skills useful:

* asking better questions;
* holding key information in mind;
* spotting patterns;
* solving unfamiliar problems;
* checking evidence;
* avoiding unsupported conclusions;
* thinking through what happens next;
* using AI without simply accepting its answers;
* applying the same thinking skill in a new context.

IQ Coach is a short, structured reasoning and thinking-performance app designed to support those skills.

It combines simple visual thinking tasks, working-memory challenges, pattern-tracking exercises and short reasoning problems. The aim is to help users practise the building blocks of analytical thinking and then check whether those skills carry over into changed examples.

For South Africa, the strongest initial offer is not “IQ training”. It is:

> A mobile-first future-skills reasoning pilot for employability, AI-era judgement and transferable problem solving.

IQ Coach is not an official IQ test, a hiring assessment, a clinical tool or a guarantee of job placement. It is a structured development tool for practising and tracking reasoning-related skills in education, training and workforce-development settings.

# 1. The South African Skills Problem

South Africa faces a difficult combination of challenges:

* high youth unemployment;
* unequal access to opportunity;
* pressure to expand digital skills;
* a need for stronger pathways from learning to work;
* growing demand for AI and data-related capability;
* a labour market that increasingly rewards adaptability, problem solving and analytical thinking.

Many programmes already focus on technical and employability skills:

* digital literacy;
* AI skills;
* coding;
* cybersecurity;
* entrepreneurship;
* work readiness;
* communication;
* CV and interview preparation;
* sector-specific training.

These are essential. But a deeper layer is often missing:

> Can the learner reason well when the problem changes?

A young person may complete a digital-skills course but still struggle with unfamiliar problem solving.
A graduate may use AI tools but accept weak answers too quickly.
A learner may understand a worked example but fail when the question is worded differently.
An employee may learn a decision framework but not apply it under pressure.
A training provider may deliver content but lack a simple way to track thinking-skill development over time.

IQ Coach is designed to support that missing layer.

It gives learners and professionals structured practice in the thinking skills that sit underneath employability, AI use and workplace judgement.

IQ Coach trains practical thinking skills that are relevant across education, employability and work.

| Skill                        | Plain-English meaning                                                       |
| ---------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Focus Under Pressure**     | Picking out important information quickly and accurately                    |
| **Working Memory**           | Holding the right information active while solving a task                   |
| **Pattern Recognition**      | Seeing how things are connected                                             |
| **Step Tracking**            | Following how a problem develops over several steps                         |
| **Next-Step Prediction**     | Thinking ahead about what should happen next                                |
| **Evidence Checking**        | Testing whether a conclusion is actually supported                          |
| **AI Judgement**             | Checking whether an AI-generated answer is useful, incomplete or misleading |
| **Reasoning Across Formats** | Applying the same thinking skill in a new type of task                      |
| **Carry-Over Score**         | Checking whether the skill still works later and in a changed context       |

For the user, the app feels simple:

> Watch the pattern.
> Hold the key information.
> Check what follows.
> Solve a short reasoning item.
> Apply the same thinking skill to a new example.
> Re-check later.

For the partner, the value is:

> A structured way to train and track thinking skills without presenting the app as a selection test or clinical tool.

# 3. What a Session Looks Like

A typical IQ Coach session takes about 15–20 minutes.

| Block              |    Time | What the participant does                                                 |
| ------------------ | ------: | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Quick focus task   | 1–2 min | Pick out key information under time pressure                              |
| Pattern task       |   3 min | Decide whether a pattern continues or breaks                              |
| Memory task        |   2 min | Remember which pieces of information belong together                      |
| Step-tracking task | 4–5 min | Follow how a pattern or problem changes across steps                      |
| Prediction task    |   5 min | Judge whether the next step still fits                                    |
| Reasoning task     | 3–5 min | Solve short logic, language, AI-output or workplace-style reasoning items |

Each session has one main thinking theme.

Examples include:

* what information matters;
* what changed;
* what follows from the evidence;
* what does not follow;
* what is missing;
* what would make this wrong;
* what happens next;
* what is only superficially similar;
* what should be checked before acting.

The rhythm is:

> Practise the skill.
> Change the example.
> Practise again.
> Apply it to a study, work or AI-use scenario.
> Re-check later.

# 4. Why This Fits South Africa

IQ Coach is especially relevant to South Africa because it can sit between three major development priorities:

## 1. AI and digital skills

South Africa needs more people who can use digital and AI tools productively. But AI skills are not only technical skills.

Users also need to know:

* what to ask;
* what to trust;
* what to check;
* what is missing;
* what assumptions are hidden;
* whether a conclusion follows;
* when human judgement is still needed.

IQ Coach can support AI-skills programmes by training the human reasoning layer.

Learners often struggle when a familiar question becomes unfamiliar. This matters for:

* matric support;
* university transition;
* TVET and CET learning;
* entrance and aptitude preparation;
* study-skills coaching;
* private tutoring;
* scholarship and bursary readiness.

IQ Coach can support learners by training the skills behind new problems:

* focus;
* working memory;
* pattern recognition;
* multi-step reasoning;
* applying the same method in a changed question.

## 3. Workforce development and employability

Employers and training providers need people who can solve problems, adapt, use evidence and learn quickly.

IQ Coach can support employability and development programmes by providing:

* short thinking practice;
* baseline and follow-up reasoning checks;
* carry-over checks;
* cohort-level learning reports;
* structured reflection prompts;
* evidence-safe progress tracking.

The core South African offer is therefore:

> A future-skills reasoning layer for education, employability and AI-era workforce development.

| **Step Tracking**            | Can the participant follow a multi-step problem?                  |
| **Next-Step Prediction**     | Can the participant think through likely consequences?            |
| **Evidence Checking**        | Can the participant tell whether a conclusion is supported?       |
| **AI Judgement**             | Can the participant review AI-generated claims carefully?         |

Participants can see:

## Partner feedback

Partners can see de-identified cohort-level trends:

* completion rates;
* engagement;
* usability;
* skill trends;
* common reasoning bottlenecks;
* carry-over patterns;
* participant-rated usefulness;
* suggested programme improvements.

The cohort report should support training design, not employee ranking.

# 8. Pilot Model

## Suggested offer

> 8-week Future Skills Reasoning Pilot

## Aim

To test whether IQ Coach helps participants:

* engage with short structured thinking practice;
* improve on app-based focus, memory and reasoning tasks;
* apply thinking skills to changed examples;
* review AI outputs more carefully;
* complete baseline and follow-up reasoning checks;
* find the practice useful for study, work or employability.

## Structure

| Phase     | Purpose                                    | Output                                             |
| --------- | ------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------- |
| Week 0    | Onboarding and baseline reasoning check    | Starting profile                                   |
| Weeks 1–2 | Focus, working memory and pattern practice | Core thinking profile                              |
| Weeks 3–4 | Evidence checking and step tracking        | Analytical-thinking practice                       |
| Weeks 5–6 | AI judgement and workplace-style scenarios | Applied reasoning profile                          |
| Week 7    | Delayed carry-over checks                  | Carry-Over Score                                   |
| Week 8    | Review and report                          | Individual summary and de-identified cohort report |

## Recommended use

Participants complete:

* 3 sessions per week;
* 15–20 minutes per session;
* one short reflection per week;
* one baseline and one follow-up reasoning benchmark.

## Optional delivery models

| Model                          | Best for                                         |
| ------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------ |
| Mobile-first self-guided pilot | Youth programmes, students, distributed learners |
| Facilitated group pilot        | TVET, university, NGO and employability cohorts  |
| Corporate L&D pilot            | Graduate and employee-development programmes     |
| AI-skills add-on               | AI-literacy and digital-skills providers         |
| Train-the-trainer model        | SETA-linked and large-scale training partners    |

# 10. Governance and Safeguards

Because this offer may be used in education, workforce-development and corporate settings, governance should be explicit.

## Recommended safeguards

* development-only use;
* no hiring or promotion decisions;
* no individual ranking reports to employers;
* no clinical or diagnostic claims;
* voluntary participation where possible;
* clear explanation of the app’s purpose;
* accessible, mobile-first design;
* data protection and privacy safeguards;
* de-identified cohort reporting by default;
* participant consent for any individual report sharing;
* fairness monitoring across language, device and demographic groups;
* separate validation before any high-stakes assessment use.

## Reporting principles

| Report type                      | Recommended use                               |
| -------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| Individual report to participant | Personal development feedback                 |
| Trainer report                   | Only with participant consent                 |
| Employer report                  | Prefer de-identified cohort trends            |
| Funder report                    | Engagement, completion and aggregate outcomes |
| Selection report                 | Not offered in pilot phase                    |

The key rule is:

> IQ Coach should be used to support development, not to screen people out.

> A non-clinical, evidence-informed thinking-skills app that helps participants practise and track reasoning skills relevant to learning, employability, AI judgement and adaptive problem solving.
